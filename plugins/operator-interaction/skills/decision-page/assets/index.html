Decision answers

For each decision, pick one option or one follow-up (the radio buttons allow only one), and add your words if you like. Each click saves at once; there is no submit button. Tell Claude in chat when you're done. The map under the progress bar lists the groups; each decision is one line in its group, and a click on the line opens its card (Expand all and Collapse all open or close every card). Use recommendation and Later beside a line answer without opening the card. A number · name is a decision: hover over it or focus it to see it, and click it to open its card; on one not on this page, a tap or click pins what it shows, and a second tap, a tap elsewhere or Escape closes it. Each line shows, after an arrow (→) and its letter, what the recommended option changes in the system the work builds (with no recommendation, each option's, in a few words); each card opens on a TLDR, then its Context (the terms it uses), then its What (what is decided; a set lists every item it acts on, and each item's outcome under each option where they differ), then, on a decision raised from a finding, its Finding (where it lives, how it happens, and its code, shown exactly as written), then, where the work states requirements, its Scope (required by a criterion, shapes the work, or outside the criteria), then, on a decision about a failure that may or may not happen, its Likelihood (how often it happens, observed or from the evidence there is) and its Best response? (whether acting here is the response that matters), then its impact, one facet a line, then one line per option, each but (z) with its Pros and Cons under it; the folds under it go from compressed to full detail. A part with a more detail button, above or inside a fold, shows more detail as short bullets when you click it or its text, and full detail in headed sections on a second click; picking an option opens its view (and closes the card's other one), and More beside an option opens or closes its view alone: its plan in brief, its Pros and Cons side by side, then its own effect, reach, undo and cost. A part with nothing to say (none) is left out. An Impact describes the system the work builds, never the review or the process; its later: line says what waiting holds up. Open questions, folded above the Impact, lists what is not known yet; Assumptions, folded under it, gives the basis of the call, its evidence, and each assumption it rests on with its tripwire, the sign that would change it. Attached, the fold after Background, holds the material a decision rests on (an excerpt, a table, check results and the like), each entry closed until you open it; a dotted link opens the attachment it names. Rejected alternatives, the last fold, lists the choices that were weighed and not offered, each with why.
